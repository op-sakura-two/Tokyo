Transit

Lines, stops and leg times, day by day

Every day starts and ends at Shinjuku. Each is a loop: out to the far stop, across to the near one, then home — you do not come back to the hotel in between. Tap Suica in and out; the fare sorts itself.
Sun Oct 11 — Haneda to Shinjuku
Recommended — Airport Limousine bus, T3 arrivals to Hyatt Regency Shinjuku. ~55 min, no transfers, no stairs, a few minutes' walk from Gracery
Rail alternative — Keikyu Airport line, T3 to Shinagawa (~13 min) → JR Yamanote to Shinjuku (~20 min). Faster on paper, worse with suitcases
Mon Oct 12 — Yanaka + Akihabara
Shinjuku → Nippori — JR Yamanote, outer loop via Ikebukuro, ~20 min. Wrong loop costs you 40 min the long way round. Yanaka Ginza is out the west exit
Nippori → Akihabara — JR Yamanote, stay on the outer loop, same direction you came in, ~8 min via Ueno
Akihabara → Shinjuku — JR Chuo-Sobu local, the yellow train, direct, ~17 min
Tue Oct 13 — Tsukiji + teamLab
Shinjuku → Tsukijishijo — Toei Oedo line, direct, ~20 min
Two different stations. You arrive at Tsukijishijo (Oedo) but leave from Tsukiji (Hibiya), a 5 min walk away
Tsukiji → Kamiyacho — Hibiya line, ~10 min, then walk to Azabudai Hills. teamLab Borderless is here, not Odaiba
Home — Hibiya from Kamiyacho to Roppongi, change to Toei Oedo to Shinjuku, ~20 min
Midday option — Nakano Broadway is JR Chuo rapid, 5 min from Shinjuku
Wed Oct 14 — DisneySea
Shinjuku → Tokyo Station — JR Chuo rapid, ~14 min
Tokyo → Maihama — JR Keiyo line, ~15 min. Add 15 min for the transfer: the Keiyo platforms are a long underground walk from the main concourse and the signage barely warns you
Maihama → Tokyo DisneySea Station — Disney Resort Line monorail from Resort Gateway, 3 stops one way round the loop, ~13 min. You cannot walk to DisneySea the way you can to Disneyland
Thu Oct 15 — Shibuya + Shimokitazawa
Shinjuku → Shibuya — JR Yamanote, 2 stops, ~7 min
Shibuya → Shimokitazawa — Keio Inokashira line, express, ~6 min
Shimokitazawa → Shinjuku — Odakyu Odawara line, express, ~10 min. Not the Keio: the Inokashira runs Shibuya to Kichijoji and never reaches Shinjuku
Golden Gai is a walk from Shinjuku station's east side
Fri Oct 16 — Kyoto
Shinjuku → Tokyo Station — JR Chuo rapid, ~14 min
Tokyo → Kyoto — Tokaido Shinkansen, Nozomi, 2h15. Reserved seats via SmartEX, both directions
Kyoto Station → Inari — JR Nara line, 2 stops, ~5 min. The shrine gate is across from the station
Fushimi Inari → Gion-Shijo — walk to Keihan Fushimi-Inari, ride the Keihan, ~10 min. Do not backtrack to Kyoto Station in between
Gion → Kyoto Station — Keihan to Shichijo then 12 min walk, or city bus 206 direct
Last usable Nozomi back is around 9pm. Do not cut it fine
Sat Oct 17 — Shinjuku to Haneda
Recommended — Airport Limousine bus from Hyatt Regency Shinjuku to Haneda T3, ~55 min, no transfers
Rail alternative — JR Yamanote to Shinagawa (~20 min) → Keikyu Airport line to Haneda T3 (~13 min)

Konbini & Drugstore

What to buy where, when something goes wrong

A Japanese 7-Eleven is not a CVS. Konbini carry hydration, cooling sheets, bandages and lozenges — but almost no actual medicine. For painkillers, cold medicine or stomach remedies you need a drugstore: Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Sundrug, Welcia, or Don Quijote. All are open late and several are within a few minutes of Gracery.
At any konbini — 7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart
Pocari Sweat / Aquarius — the default rehydration drink. Jet lag, heat, hangover, too much walking
Netsusama Sheet (熱さまシート) — cooling gel forehead strips. Fever, headache, a bad night
Nodoame (のど飴) — throat lozenges. Dry plane air, early cold
Bandages and blister pads — small selection, enough for a hot spot mid-day
Hokkairo (ホッカイロ) — stick-on heat packs. Sore shoulders and legs after a long day
Ukon no Chikara / Hepalyse — turmeric shots taken before drinking. Jess only
Umeboshi onigiri, miso soup, plain rice — the local answer to an unsettled stomach
Masks — normal to wear when you have a cough. Nobody will look twice
Drugstore only — Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Sundrug, Don Quijote
Loxonin S — loxoprofen. The strong Japanese painkiller, roughly prescription-grade elsewhere. Headache, sore knees and feet
Eve / Bufferin — ibuprofen and aspirin equivalents, gentler than Loxonin
Pabron Gold — the standard cold and flu powder
Ohta's Isan (太田胃散) — antacid powder for heartburn and overeating
Seirogan (正露丸) — traditional anti-diarrheal. Smells awful, works
Gaster 10 — famotidine, for acid stomach
Blister plasters — the real ones, in the foot care aisle. Buy these on day one, not day five
Decongestant — buy it there, do not pack it
Contac 600 Plus  コンタック600プラス  — the direct Sudafed substitute. Pseudoephedrine is legal to buy inside Japan, just not to bring in
Stona Gel Sinus EX  ストナジェルサイナスEX  — stands in for Advil Cold & Sinus
Tylenol A  タイレノールA  — plain acetaminophen, the stand-in for Tylenol Cold
Nasal spray — clears congestion faster than anything oral. Most cheap OTC sprays there are decongestant-type: three days maximum or you get rebound congestion worse than you started with
Show the Japanese name on your phone at the counter. Matsumoto Kiyoshi or Sundrug, both minutes from Gracery
Likely maladies on this trip
Blisters — Fushimi Inari and DisneySea are both long days on your feet. Tape hot spots before they break
Jet lag — 13 hours. Daylight on Oct 11 and no nap past 8pm is the whole treatment
Dehydration — easy to miss when it is not hot. One Pocari a day
Dry throat and colds — aircon, planes, crowds. Lozenges early beats Pabron later
Upset stomach — unfamiliar food plus exhaustion. Plain rice, miso, Ohta's Isan
Before you pack
Japan treats pseudoephedrine as a stimulant raw material under the Stimulants Control Act. Sudafed, Actifed, Vicks inhalers, NyQuil, Tylenol Cold, Advil Cold & Sinus and Dristan Sinus are all prohibited imports. There are no exceptions, even for medication legally bought at home, and customs does not make on-the-spot humanitarian exceptions. Codeine products and stimulant prescriptions such as Adderall are also banned. Check the active ingredient panel, not the brand name — that is where people get caught. Plain ibuprofen, acetaminophen and antihistamines are fine, up to a two-month supply. Prescriptions travel in their original labelled container with a copy of the script.

The brand's largest flagship in the world, opened July 2026. Four floors, 1,837 sqm, yellow concrete facade. 2F and 3F carry the NIPPON MADE series, the Astro Boy collaboration and a deep Mexico 66 range, plus Shinjuku-exclusive shirts. Duty-free counters and multilingual staff on site, so Omotesando is not worth the trip any more.
